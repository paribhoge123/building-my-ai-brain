# Day 046: Regularization (L1 & L2)

## What I Learned

Today I learned about **Regularization**, a fundamental technique used to reduce overfitting in Machine Learning models.

When a model becomes too complex, it may memorize the training data instead of learning meaningful patterns. Regularization addresses this problem by adding a penalty to the model's loss function, encouraging smaller weights and simpler models that generalize better to unseen data.

The two most common regularization techniques are **L1 Regularization (Lasso)** and **L2 Regularization (Ridge)**.

---

## What is Regularization?

Regularization is a method used to prevent overfitting by penalizing overly large model weights during training.

By discouraging complex models, regularization improves generalization and helps models perform better on unseen data.

---

## Why Do We Need Regularization?

Without regularization:

* Models may memorize noise in the training data.
* Large weights make predictions overly sensitive.
* Overfitting becomes more likely.

With regularization:

* Models become simpler.
* Generalization improves.
* Overfitting is reduced.

---

## L1 Regularization (Lasso)

L1 Regularization adds the absolute values of model weights to the loss function.

Its key characteristics include:

* Can reduce some weights exactly to zero.
* Performs automatic feature selection.
* Produces sparse models.
* Useful when many features are irrelevant.

---

## L2 Regularization (Ridge)

L2 Regularization adds the squared values of model weights to the loss function.

Its key characteristics include:

* Shrinks weights toward zero without eliminating them.
* Retains all features.
* Produces smoother and more stable models.
* Commonly used in many Machine Learning algorithms.

---

## Lambda (λ)

Lambda controls the strength of regularization.

* A small λ applies weak regularization, allowing the model to remain more complex.
* A large λ applies strong regularization, simplifying the model but potentially causing underfitting if set too high.

Selecting an appropriate λ is essential for achieving good performance.

---

## Elastic Net

Elastic Net combines both L1 and L2 Regularization.

It benefits from L1's ability to perform feature selection while also leveraging L2's ability to stabilize model weights.

---

## Advantages

* Reduces overfitting.
* Improves generalization.
* Produces simpler models.
* Prevents excessively large feature weights.
* Enhances model robustness.

---

## Limitations

* Selecting an appropriate regularization strength can be challenging.
* Excessive regularization may lead to underfitting.
* Training accuracy may decrease slightly as model complexity is reduced.

---

## Real-World Applications

Regularization is widely used in:

* Linear Regression
* Logistic Regression
* Neural Networks
* Deep Learning
* Medical diagnosis
* Financial forecasting

---

## L1 vs L2 Regularization

Although both reduce overfitting, they differ in how they treat model weights.

* **L1 Regularization (Lasso)** can eliminate unimportant features by setting some weights to zero.
* **L2 Regularization (Ridge)** reduces the magnitude of all weights while keeping every feature in the model.

The choice depends on the dataset and the problem being solved.

---

## How This Fits Into the Bigger Picture

Regularization builds upon the Bias-Variance Tradeoff by providing a practical method for controlling model complexity.

It enables Machine Learning models to avoid memorizing training data while maintaining strong predictive performance on unseen data, making it an essential tool in modern Machine Learning workflows.

---

## Key Takeaways

* Regularization reduces overfitting by penalizing large weights.
* L1 Regularization performs feature selection by setting some weights to zero.
* L2 Regularization shrinks weights without removing features.
* Lambda controls the strength of regularization.
* Elastic Net combines the advantages of both L1 and L2.
* Regularization improves model generalization and robustness.

---

## Why This Matters

Regularization is one of the most widely used techniques in Machine Learning because it helps models balance complexity and generalization. Understanding L1, L2, and Elastic Net is essential for building reliable predictive models that perform well on real-world data.
