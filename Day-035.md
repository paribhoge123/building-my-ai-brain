# Day 035: Decision Trees

## What I Learned

Today I learned about **Decision Trees**, one of the most intuitive supervised Machine Learning algorithms.

Decision Trees make predictions by asking a sequence of questions about the input features. Instead of learning mathematical equations like Linear Regression or comparing neighboring data points like KNN, a Decision Tree follows a flowchart-like structure where each decision gradually narrows down the possible outcomes.

Because of their simplicity and interpretability, Decision Trees are widely used for both classification and regression problems.

---

## What is a Decision Tree?

A Decision Tree is a supervised learning algorithm that predicts an output by repeatedly splitting the dataset based on feature values.

Each split is chosen to separate the data into increasingly pure groups until a final prediction can be made.

Decision Trees can be used for both:

* Classification
* Regression

---

## Structure of a Decision Tree

A Decision Tree consists of four main components.

### Root Node

The starting point of the tree where the first decision is made.

---

### Internal Nodes

Intermediate decision points that further divide the data.

---

### Branches

Connections representing the possible outcomes of each decision.

---

### Leaf Nodes

The final nodes that contain the prediction or output.

---

## How Decision Trees Work

The algorithm begins with the complete dataset and searches for the feature that best separates the data.

After selecting the best split, the dataset is divided into smaller subsets.

This process continues recursively until stopping criteria are met or the data becomes sufficiently pure.

The final prediction is obtained from the leaf node reached after following the sequence of decisions.

---

## Splitting

Splitting is the process of dividing the dataset into smaller groups based on a feature.

A good split creates groups that are more homogeneous and easier to classify.

The objective is to reduce the mixing of different classes within each node.

---

## Purity

Purity measures how similar the data points within a node are.

A node containing mostly one class is considered highly pure.

Decision Trees attempt to maximize purity after every split to improve prediction accuracy.

---

## Measuring Split Quality

To determine the best split, Decision Trees commonly use impurity measures such as:

* Gini Impurity
* Entropy (Information Gain)

These metrics help the algorithm identify the feature that separates the data most effectively.

---

## Classification and Regression

For classification tasks, the leaf node predicts a category such as Spam or Not Spam.

For regression tasks, the leaf node predicts a continuous numerical value, often by averaging the target values within that node.

---

## Advantages

* Easy to understand and interpret.
* Can solve both classification and regression problems.
* Handles nonlinear relationships.
* Requires minimal data preprocessing.
* Produces explainable decision paths.

---

## Limitations

* Can easily overfit the training data.
* Sensitive to small changes in the dataset.
* Large trees may become complex and difficult to interpret.

---

## Real-World Applications

Decision Trees are widely used in:

* Loan approval systems
* Medical diagnosis
* Fraud detection
* Credit risk assessment
* Customer segmentation
* Business decision support

---

## How This Fits Into the Bigger Picture

Decision Trees represent a different approach to Machine Learning.

Unlike Linear Regression, which learns equations, or KNN, which compares neighboring examples, Decision Trees learn a hierarchy of decision rules that progressively narrow down the prediction.

They also serve as the foundation for powerful ensemble algorithms such as **Random Forest** and **Gradient Boosted Trees**, which combine multiple Decision Trees to achieve significantly higher accuracy.

---

## Key Takeaways

* Decision Trees are supervised learning algorithms.
* They make predictions through a sequence of decision rules.
* A tree consists of root nodes, internal nodes, branches, and leaf nodes.
* Splitting divides data into smaller groups.
* Purity measures how homogeneous a node is.
* Gini Impurity and Entropy are commonly used to evaluate split quality.
* Decision Trees support both classification and regression.

---

## Why This Matters

Decision Trees are among the most interpretable Machine Learning algorithms. Their ability to represent decisions in a clear, logical structure makes them valuable for both practical applications and understanding more advanced ensemble methods.
