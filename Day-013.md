Day 013: Large Language Models (LLMs) – How They Work

What I Learned:

Today I learned about Large Language Models (LLMs), the technology behind modern AI systems like ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and many other AI assistants.
An LLM is a Transformer-based neural network trained on massive amounts of text data to understand and generate human language.
At its core, an LLM performs one simple task:
Predict the next token in a sequence.
By repeatedly predicting one token after another, it can generate complete sentences, answer questions, write code, summarize documents, and perform many other language-related tasks.

Why Are They Called Large Language Models?

The word Large refers to two important aspects.

Large Training Data

LLMs are trained on enormous collections of text from books, articles, websites, research papers, documentation, and code.

Learning from diverse text sources helps the model understand grammar, language patterns, facts, reasoning, and programming syntax.

Large Number of Parameters

Parameters are the weights learned during training.

Modern LLMs contain billions of parameters, allowing them to capture highly complex relationships within language.

How an LLM Processes Text

An LLM follows several important steps before generating a response.

Input Text
      ↓
Tokenization
      ↓
Embeddings
      ↓
Positional Encoding
      ↓
Transformer Layers
      ↓
Next Token Prediction
      ↓
Generated Response

Each step contributes to the model's understanding of language.

Tokenization

Computers cannot directly understand words.

The input text is divided into smaller units called tokens.

A token may represent:

A complete word
Part of a word
A punctuation mark
Special symbols

The model processes these tokens instead of raw text.

Embeddings

After tokenization, each token is converted into a numerical vector called an embedding.

Embeddings capture the semantic meaning of words.

Words with similar meanings tend to have similar vector representations, helping the model understand relationships between different words.

Positional Encoding

Transformers process all tokens simultaneously.

To understand the order of words, positional information is added to each token.

This enables the model to distinguish between sentences where the same words appear in different positions.

Self-Attention

Self-Attention is the core mechanism of an LLM.

Instead of processing words one by one, every token can interact with every other token in the sentence.

This allows the model to identify which words are most relevant when interpreting the meaning of the input.

As a result, the model develops a much stronger understanding of context than previous neural network architectures.

Next Token Prediction

After processing the input, the model predicts the most likely next token.

For example:

Input:
I love artificial

Prediction:
intelligence

Once a token is generated, it is added to the input, and the prediction process repeats until the response is complete.

This repeated prediction process is how LLMs generate human-like text.

Training an LLM

During training, the model repeatedly performs the following cycle:

Input Text
      ↓
Prediction
      ↓
Loss Function
      ↓
Backpropagation
      ↓
Gradient Descent
      ↓
Updated Parameters

By repeating this process over massive datasets, the model gradually improves its ability to predict language accurately.

Pretraining and Fine-Tuning

LLMs are generally developed in two stages.

Pretraining

The model learns general language patterns from large amounts of text by predicting the next token.

Fine-Tuning

The pretrained model is further trained on task-specific or domain-specific datasets to improve its performance for particular applications, such as coding, customer support, or medical assistance.

Inference

After training is complete, the model enters the inference stage.

During inference, the model no longer updates its parameters.

Instead, it uses the knowledge learned during training to generate responses for new user inputs.

Key Takeaways
LLMs are Transformer-based neural networks.
Their primary objective is to predict the next token.
Text is converted into tokens before processing.
Embeddings represent the semantic meaning of tokens.
Self-Attention enables the model to understand context.
LLMs are trained using Forward Propagation, Loss Functions, Backpropagation, and Gradient Descent.
Pretraining teaches general language understanding, while Fine-Tuning specializes the model for specific tasks.
Why This Matters

Large Language Models have transformed the field of Artificial Intelligence by enabling machines to generate fluent, context-aware, and human-like language. They now power applications ranging from conversational assistants and code generation to research tools and content creation. Understanding how LLMs work provides the foundation for building modern AI applications.
